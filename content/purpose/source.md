## PAGE 1: What Is My Purpose?

**Title tag:** What Is My Purpose? A Plain-Language Guide to Finding Yours
**Meta description:** Wondering what your purpose is? Learn what life purpose really means, why it feels hard to name, and how to start finding yours today.

# What Is My Purpose?

If you've ever asked yourself this question, you're not behind and you're not broken. You're paying attention.

Most people ask it at a turning point: after a loss, a job change, a season of burnout, or a quiet moment when life looks fine on paper but feels a little hollow. The question isn't a sign something is wrong. It's a sign something in you wants to be lived more fully.

## What "purpose" actually means

Purpose is the reason behind your effort. It's the thread that connects what you care about, what you're good at, and who you want to help or become.

It is not a single job title. It is not one perfect answer you find on a mountaintop. For most people, purpose is a direction that gets clearer as you move toward it.

## Purpose, passion, and calling

These words get mixed up, so here's a simple way to separate them:

- **Passion** is what energizes you.
- **Calling** is what you feel pulled to answer.
- **Purpose** is the larger "why" that can hold both, and it can show up in many different roles over a lifetime.

You can have purpose without a dramatic calling. Many people live purposefully in ordinary, steady ways.

## Why it feels so hard to name

A few honest reasons:

1. **You've been doing what was needed, not what was true.** Years of responsibility can bury your own preferences.
2. **You're looking for one big answer.** Purpose usually shows up in pieces.
3. **You've been told to follow your passion,** when what you needed was to notice your patterns.
4. **You're tired.** It's hard to hear yourself when you're running on empty.

## Three clues hiding in your own life

You don't need to invent your purpose. You can notice it.

**What do you keep returning to?** Look at the topics, problems, and people you come back to again and again, even when no one is asking you to.

**What do people bring to you?** The questions friends and coworkers ask you often point to a strength you treat as ordinary.

**What stays with you?** Think of moments when you felt most like yourself. What were you doing, and who were you doing it for?

## A starting exercise

Take ten minutes with a pen and paper. Finish these sentences without editing yourself:

- I feel most alive when...
- The problem I can't stop thinking about is...
- People thank me for...
- If fear weren't in the way, I would...

Read your answers out loud. Circle any word that shows up more than once. That's your first clue.

## PAGE 2: How to Identify Your Personal Values

**Slug:** `/purpose/personal-values`
**Title tag:** How to Identify Your Personal Values (Simple 4-Step Exercise)
**Meta description:** Not sure what your core values are? Use this simple four-step exercise to identify your personal values and start making decisions that feel right.

# How to Identify Your Personal Values

Your values are the things you refuse to trade away, even when it would be easier. When you're living by them, life feels steady. When you're not, you feel restless, resentful, or worn down without knowing why.

Knowing your values makes decisions simpler. It's part of finding your [purpose](/purpose/what-is-my-purpose), and it's the first thing to get clear on.

## What personal values are (and aren't)

Values are not rules, goals, or what you think you *should* care about. They are what you actually protect with your time, money, and energy.

A goal is something you finish. A value is something you keep living. "Run a 5K" is a goal. "Health" is a value.

## The 4-step exercise

### Step 1: Look at your peak moments
Write down three times you felt proud, fulfilled, or deeply like yourself. Be specific. What was happening? Who was there?

### Step 2: Look at your frustrations
Now write down three times you felt angry, drained, or resentful. Frustration is often a value being stepped on. If unfairness makes you furious, fairness may be a core value. If chaos wears you out, order or peace may be.

### Step 3: Name what was present
For each moment, ask what was being honored or violated. Use plain words: honesty, family, freedom, growth, faith, creativity, service, security, beauty, learning, courage.

### Step 4: Narrow to your top five
Circle the words that repeat. Then narrow to five. If it's hard to cut, imagine you could only keep three. The ones you won't give up are your core values.

## How to know you've got the right ones

A true value passes three tests:

- You'd keep living by it even if no one noticed.
- It has shown up in the choices you've already made.
- Living against it costs you something, like sleep, peace, or energy.

## Values in conflict

Sometimes two values pull against each other, like security and freedom. That doesn't mean one is wrong. It means you're human. Ask which value matters more *in this season*, not forever.

## Using your values

Put your five values somewhere you'll see them. Before a big decision, ask: "Which option honors my values?" It won't make every choice easy, but it makes it honest.

## PAGE 3: How to Write a Personal Mission Statement

**Slug:** `/purpose/personal-mission-statement`
**Title tag:** How to Write a Personal Mission Statement (Template + Examples)
**Meta description:** Learn how to write a personal mission statement that sounds like you. Includes a simple template, examples, and common mistakes to avoid.

# How to Write a Personal Mission Statement

A personal mission statement is a short statement of who you want to be, what you want to do, and who it's for. It's not a slogan. It's a compass you can check when life gets noisy.

A good one is short enough to remember and true enough to guide a decision.

## Before you write

Have your [personal values](/purpose/personal-values) in front of you, plus your answers from the [starter exercise](/purpose/what-is-my-purpose). Your mission is built from material you already have.

## A simple template

> I exist to **[action]** for **[who]** so that **[result]**, guided by **[2 or 3 values]**.

Fill in each blank:

- **Action:** a verb like teach, build, protect, create, heal, guide, serve.
- **Who:** the people, community, or cause you care about, including yourself.
- **Result:** what changes because of your effort.
- **Values:** the ones you won't compromise.

## Examples

These are for inspiration only. Yours should sound like you.

- *"I exist to help working mothers feel less alone, guided by honesty, humor, and care."*
- *"I build practical tools that give small business owners their time back, guided by integrity and simplicity."*
- *"I create spaces where people feel safe to be curious, guided by patience and respect."*

## Five tips for a statement that works

1. **Keep it to one or two sentences.** If you can't say it from memory, it's too long.
2. **Use your own words.** Skip phrases you'd never say out loud.
3. **Focus on impact, not title.** Titles change. Impact travels with you.
4. **Write a rough draft first.** Your first version is for finding the shape.
5. **Revisit it once a year.** Your mission can grow as you do.

## Common mistakes

- Writing what sounds impressive instead of what's true
- Making it so broad it fits anyone
- Copying someone else's
- Treating it as finished forever

## Test your draft

Read it and ask:

- Does this sound like me?
- Could this help me say yes or no to something this week?
- Does it make me feel steady, not pressured?

If you answered yes, you have a working draft.

## Take it for a test drive

A statement on paper is just a start. If you're turning yours into a business or an offer, the Clarity Check can show you what to work on next across your direction, systems, and AI. If your mission is personal, try living with it for a week and notice where it helps you say yes or no.

## PAGE 4: Life Purpose vs. Goals

**Slug:** `/purpose/life-purpose-vs-goals`
**Title tag:** Life Purpose vs. Goals: What's the Difference and Why It Matters
**Meta description:** Purpose and goals are not the same thing. Learn the difference, how they work together, and how to set life goals that actually fit you.

# Life Purpose vs. Goals: What's the Difference?

Many people set goal after goal and still feel something is missing. Often the missing piece isn't more effort. It's direction.

## The short answer

**Purpose is the "why." Goals are the "what."**

Purpose is the ongoing reason behind your efforts. Goals are specific, measurable things you aim to achieve. Purpose doesn't get completed. Goals do.

## A simple comparison

| | Purpose | Goals |
|---|---|---|
| Question it answers | Why am I doing this? | What am I trying to reach? |
| Timeframe | Long-term, ongoing | Short or medium-term |
| Can it be finished? | No | Yes |
| Example | Help people feel less alone | Host a monthly support circle |

## Why goals without purpose go sideways

You can hit a goal and feel empty. That often happens when the goal came from someone else's expectations, from comparison, or from habit. You did it right, but it wasn't yours.

## Why purpose without goals goes nowhere

The opposite problem is just as real. You can feel deeply called and still stay stuck, because "make a difference" doesn't tell you what to do on Tuesday. Purpose needs goals to become real.

## How they work together

Think of purpose as the direction and goals as the steps.

1. **Start with your purpose** ([need help?](/purpose/what-is-my-purpose)).
2. **Choose a season.** What matters most over the next 90 days?
3. **Set one to three goals** that move you in that direction.
4. **Break each goal into a small next action** you can do this week.

## Questions to check a goal

Before you commit to a goal, ask:

- Does this connect to something I care about?
- Would I still want this if no one knew I did it?
- Does it fit my current season, or am I forcing it?

If the answer is no, you may have found a goal worth dropping. That's not failure. It's clarity.

## Where to go next

Knowing the difference is useful. If your goals are about your work or a business, the Clarity Check looks at your direction, systems, and AI to point you toward what to focus on next. If your goals are personal, the questions above are a good way to check each one against your purpose.

## PAGE 5: Self-Reflection Questions That Actually Help

**Slug:** `/purpose/self-reflection-questions`
**Title tag:** Self-Reflection Questions That Actually Help (And How to Use Them)
**Meta description:** Self-reflection works when the questions are good. Get thoughtful prompts for clarity, direction, and growth, plus a simple way to use them.

# Self-Reflection Questions That Actually Help

Self-reflection is simply pausing to look honestly at your life. Done well, it builds clarity, self-trust, and better decisions. Done poorly, it turns into rumination, going in circles without getting anywhere.

The difference is in the questions and in how you ask them.

## How to reflect without spiraling

- **Ask "what" and "how," not "why am I like this?"** "What do I need right now?" moves you forward. "Why am I always like this?" keeps you stuck.
- **Set a time limit.** Ten to fifteen minutes is plenty.
- **Write it down.** Thoughts loop in your head and settle on paper.
- **End with one action.** Reflection without a next step is just worry.

## Questions for everyday check-ins

- What gave me energy this week? What drained it?
- What am I avoiding, and what would be a small first step?
- What am I proud of that I haven't acknowledged?
- What do I need more of? Less of?

## Questions for clarity and direction

- If nothing about my situation changed, would I be okay in a year?
- What would I do if I trusted myself fully?
- What am I tolerating that I no longer want to?
- Where am I living by someone else's expectations?

## Questions about your values

(See also: [How to Identify Your Personal Values](/purpose/personal-values))

- When did I feel most like myself recently?
- What do I get angry about, and what does that say about what I protect?
- What would I want said about how I lived?

## Questions for hard seasons

- What is this season trying to teach me?
- Who or what could support me right now?
- What is within my control today?

## A simple weekly practice

1. Pick a quiet time and place.
2. Choose three questions from the lists above.
3. Write for ten minutes without editing.
4. Underline one sentence that surprises you.
5. Turn it into one small action for the coming week.

## PAGE 6: How to Find Your Purpose When You Feel Stuck

**Slug:** `/purpose/find-purpose-when-stuck`
**Title tag:** How to Find Your Purpose When You Feel Stuck or Lost
**Meta description:** Feeling stuck, lost, or unsure what you're meant to do? Here are gentle, practical steps to find direction again, one small step at a time.

# How to Find Your Purpose When You Feel Stuck

First, take a breath. Feeling stuck doesn't mean you've failed or that your purpose is out of reach. It usually means you've outgrown something, you're exhausted, or you've been carrying too much for too long.

You don't have to figure everything out today. You only need the next honest step.

## Why people get stuck

Often it's one of these:

- **Burnout.** When you're depleted, nothing feels clear.
- **A season of change.** A loss, a move, or a role ending can leave you without a map.
- **Shame or comparison.** It's hard to hear your own voice under someone else's timeline.
- **Too many options.** Sometimes the problem isn't no direction. It's too many.
- **Waiting to feel ready.** Clarity usually comes after movement, not before.

Notice which one sounds like you. Naming it takes some of the weight off.

## Seven gentle steps

### 1. Rest before you decide
If you're running on empty, start there. Sleep, food, a walk. Clear thinking needs a rested mind.

### 2. Name what's true right now
Write one sentence: "Right now I feel ______ and I need ______." Be honest, not impressive.

### 3. Look backward for clues
When have you felt most alive or useful? Write down three moments. The common thread is a clue. (The [starter exercise](/purpose/what-is-my-purpose) can help.)

### 4. Pay attention to what bothers you
What problem do you notice that others seem to walk past? That is often tied to your purpose.

### 5. Take one small experiment
Not a big leap. Try a conversation, a class, a volunteer shift, or a one-hour project. Let the experience teach you.

### 6. Talk to someone who knows you
Ask a trusted person, "What do you see me being good at?" Their answer may surprise you.

### 7. Choose a direction, not a destination
You don't need the final answer. Pick the next direction that feels a little more true, and walk toward it.

## What to remember

- Feeling lost is a normal part of purpose, not a sign you're missing it.
- Small steps count.
- You're allowed to change your mind.

If this has been heavy for a long time, or you're feeling hopeless, please reach out to someone you trust or a mental health professional. You deserve real support, and this page isn't a substitute for it.

## You don't have to do this alone

There's no rush here, and nothing you have to do today. The steps above are enough for now. When you're ready, choose the next one that feels a little more true and start there.

## PAGE 7: How to Turn Your Purpose Into Action (or a Business)

**Slug:** `/purpose/purpose-into-action`
**Title tag:** How to Turn Your Purpose Into Action, a Career, or a Business
**Meta description:** Know your purpose but not sure what to do with it? Learn how to turn it into action, a career shift, or a purpose-driven business, step by step.

# How to Turn Your Purpose Into Action

You've done the reflection. You have a sense of what matters. Now comes the part many people stall on: doing something with it.

Here's the good news. Action doesn't have to mean quitting your job or launching a company. It means taking the next real step in the direction you've named.

## Start with a small, visible step

Momentum matters more than perfection. Choose one action you can finish in a week:

- Have a conversation with someone doing work you admire.
- Offer your skill to one person for free or at a low cost.
- Share a short piece of writing or a video about what you care about.
- Block two hours weekly for your purpose work.

## Three paths from purpose to action

### 1. Bring purpose into your current life
You may not need a change. Ask how your current job, home, or relationships could carry more of what matters.

### 2. Shift your career
Look for roles where your values and strengths are used. Test them first through projects, volunteering, or short conversations before leaving anything.

### 3. Build something of your own
If you're drawn to starting a business, begin by solving one real problem for one real person. Don't start with a logo or a perfect plan.

## If you're thinking about a business

A purpose-driven business starts with three questions:

1. **Who is the specific person I want to help?**
2. **What problem do they have that I can help solve?**
3. **How will I be able to keep this going?** (Time, money, energy.)

Many people have a clear purpose and still struggle because nothing holds the work together. That's where systems come in.

## Why systems come next

A purpose is a direction. A system is what makes progress repeatable even on busy or low-energy days. A simple system might be a weekly plan, a short list of priorities, or a repeatable way to reach people.

Once you have a system, tools like AI can take real work off your plate: drafting, organizing, planning, following up. It works best when it supports a purpose and a structure you already have. That's the order that works: **Soul, then Systems, then AI.**

## A simple 30-day plan

- **Week 1:** Write your purpose in one sentence. Choose one goal that fits it.
- **Week 2:** Take one small action toward that goal and notice how it feels.
- **Week 3:** Build one simple routine to keep going.
- **Week 4:** Look back. What worked? What do you want more of?
