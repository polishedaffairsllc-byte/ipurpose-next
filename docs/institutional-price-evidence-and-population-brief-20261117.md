## 1. Recommended initial price and deliverable
**Standard paid eight-week pilot: $7,500 USD total for up to 40 adult participants**, virtual/blended using existing capabilities (equivalent $187.50 per person at full participation; $937.50 a week). Do not sell as 40 app logins. Sell as a guided decision-to-action program.

Included subject to feasibility and final statement of work:
- institution discovery/scoping call, cohort onboarding and one orientation;
- participant access to the existing suitable iPurpose experience after QA/privacy gate;
- eight-week facilitated follow-through using approved learning prompts, optional responsible AI assistance, and one participant-owned Decision-to-Action Plan;
- at least 2 facilitated live virtual touchpoints (orientation/midpoint or final); additional support cadence and facilitator hours contractually capped;
- end-of-pilot anonymized aggregate progress and lessons-learned report; one decision meeting;
- standard support for access and reasonable participant questions; no 1:1 clinical/career counseling, placement promise, special data integrations or in-person travel.

**Low-friction paid entry:** 90-minute institutional decision-to-action workshop + materials and action template, up to 40 participants, **$1,500 USD** virtual. No outcome study/report or 8-week support; the institution can expand later only after approval. This is an independent product, not a discount on the existing University of Ghana free pilot.

**Optional repeat engagement (not yet quoted):** determine actual direct delivery cost, repeatable outcomes, required hours, procurement budget and desired margin before suggesting annual or per-seat licensing. No established recurring revenue yet.

### Why $7,500 is a plausible starting hypothesis
Public examples for different (not directly comparable) institutional training include ARIS/University of Missouri institutional training at $5,000 for a half-day workshop and $10,000 for a full-day workshop, https://researchinsociety.org/training/aris-institutional-training/ . Fort Hays State University's public 2026 training schedule shows multi-hour training for groups at differing prices; exact format and provider quality differ. This validates that institutional program budgets can accommodate thousands of dollars, **not** that iPurpose specifically commands $7,500.
No institutional buyer has validated pricing. Do not describe it as market rate.

## 2. External evidence that demonstrates a problem — not iPurpose efficacy
- **Strada, October 2026: Quality Coaching and Guidance**, https://www.strada.org/reports/quality-coaching-and-guidance-experiences-sources-and-student-confidence . Only 3% of sampled seniors at public bachelor-granting schools and 7% of sampled public two-year students reported receiving all three quality-guidance elements. The elements are personalized planning guidance, timely labor-market information, and goal/obstacle support. The study's sample and self-reports matter. This is the most direct evidence of a composite guidance gap.
- **NACE, October 2026: State of Career Readiness**, https://www.naceweb.org/career-readiness/trends-and-predictions/the-state-of-career-readiness-the-first-national-benchmarks-study-of-americas-college-students . More than 17,000 self-rated students at 57 institutions; career/self-development is the competency requiring most help. NACE's validated assessment tool is not iPurpose's and must not be claimed as our psychometric validation.
- **Strada October 2026 qualitative labor-market info study**, https://www.strada.org/reports/increasing-student-access-to-labor-market-information . 37 interviews suggest access and interpretation of career information are fragmented even where services exist.
- **Strada/Burning Glass Talent Disrupted 2024**, https://www.strada.org/reports/talent-disrupted . 52% of bachelor's graduates one year after graduation employed in jobs not typically requiring a degree. **Not** evidence of unhappiness, purposelessness or the effect of career coaching.
- **Gallup/Lumina, 2026**, https://news.gallup.com/poll/702284/college-students-grads-strong-career-value-degree.aspx . Roughly 9/10 current U.S. associate/bachelor students report confidence in skills/job benefits of degree; many graduates value degree. Prevent narrative that everyone is unhappy or college fails.
- **Gallup alumni purpose research, 2020**, https://news.gallup.com/poll/312623/purpose-pay-define-good-job-college-grads.aspx . Meaning/mission is a reason graduates call their first job good, but results are older and do not establish universal need for iPurpose.
**Conclusion:** sell decision-to-action, navigation and student agency as an optional supported experience; never diagnose 'out of purpose' from a degree, employment status or demographic identity.

## 3. Who measures the population, and how to define it ethically
| Population/transition moment | Existing steward of aggregate information | What can be observed | Who to ask to sponsor a cohort | Safe participation offer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-year college entrants with unclear pathways | Institutional Research, Advising, Orientation / first-year seminars; NSSE/BCSSE if administered | career plans, relevance, resources, confidence, engagement; not 'purpose diagnosis' | First-Year Experience director, Career Center director, Student Success VP | opt-in module for all participants; pre/post decision confidence and concrete plan |
| Seniors transitioning to workforce | Career Center, Institutional Research, NACE First-Destination Survey; alumni outcomes | career plans, knowledge rate, employment/education outcome, preparedness | Career Center director, department chair/capstone program, Provost/student-success budgets | opt-in class/capstone module, document one decision, 14-day next step |
| Students changing majors or returning | Registrar/advising/student success (restricted records) | major change, stopout/reenrollment, appointment utilization; not presumed identity issue | Advising/retention lead | neutral opt-in transition workshop, not targeting based on sensitive risk scores |
| Dislocated/laid-off workers | GDOL UI/RESEA, U.S. DOL ETA/WIOA, local workforce boards | claimant counts, RESEA activity, enrolled/served/exited, employment after exit; confidential individual records | WorkSource local workforce development board, nonprofit contractor, state agency program manager | optional 'career transition to action' module alongside existing reemployment services |
| Workforce nonprofit clients | Program's case-management & outcome reporting / funder | service access, training attendance, plans and employment milestones | Program director + COO/executive sponsor/foundation funder | optional staff-supervised follow-through, not claiming employment causation |

**Campus instruments & stewards:**
- Indiana University NSSE 2025 Career and Workforce Preparation module directly asks students about clarity of career plans, relevance of studies, understanding required career skills, access to support and confidence. https://nsse.indiana.edu/nsse/survey-instruments/topical-modules/work-career-prep.html . Survey text is copyrighted; adapt original questions only when permission/licensing is appropriate, else author distinct originals.
- NACE First-Destination survey reports outcomes within 6 months, not purpose or fulfillment. https://www.naceweb.org/job-market/graduate-outcomes/first-destination/
- Georgia Higher Learning and Earnings dashboards show earnings after college by degree/major. Public aggregate data, not mental state. https://goews.georgia.gov/dashboards-data-report-card/data-dashboards/higher-learning-higher-earning .
- U.S. DOL ETA WIOA state performance: counts and outcomes for adults and dislocated workers, not personal identity. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/performance/results/states
- GDOL UI RESEA details existing unemployment claimant support, assessment, resume, work-search and planning obligations, not an empty service gap. https://dol.georgia.gov/ui-resea-program

**No need for personally identifiable lists:** ask program sponsor to offer a voluntary, adult-only workshop to an appropriate existing cohort or course and measure consenting participation on a new non-diagnostic reflection. Do not request claimant or student contact lists; where selection uses restricted records it remains entirely within authorized institution staff and policies.

## 4. Institution-specific need discovery — proactive rather than waiting for unhappiness
Before a sale, ask institutional buyer for **existing aggregate** indicators:
1. Among students with degree plans, what share report clear career plans and know where to get assistance?
2. How many career center touches end with an action plan and what is completed after 2–4 weeks?
3. Which units/courses already perform related work, and where do students still get stuck?
4. What career-readiness/outcomes KPIs and grant deliverables are underperforming?
5. Which unit owns a budget for gap-filling support, and how does it buy services?

**Proposed voluntary 5-item iPurpose baseline, not a validated screening tool, 1–5 agree scale:**
- I can explain one next academic/career decision I need to make.
- I can identify one credible source of information relevant to that choice.
- I know how to compare two options and what trade-offs matter to me.
- I can identify a specific action I can take within the next 14 days.
- I know whom to ask for help if I get stuck.
Optional qualitative: "What concrete step are you currently trying to take?" Avoid collection of sensitive counseling/health data. Repeat baseline at end only with appropriate consent, measure plan completion and action, report missingness and selection bias. Do not call this a scientifically validated purpose score.

**Cohort invitation can be universal** for a first-year seminar, senior capstone, career center program, or WIOA partner's existing approved participants. That avoids stigmatizing labels and data-sharing complexity. The sponsor must approve and distribute it; no direct outreach to individual claimants or students without authorization.

## 5. Georgia unemployment/state procurement — pathway
The GDOL RESEA program already provides employment services and refers claimants into WIOA/WorkSource, with selected participant requirements. Do **not** pitch a replacement to UI claim processing. Position optional action-plan follow-through alongside existing employment/career services.

**Buyers**: program manager for reemployment services, local Workforce Development Board/WorkSource contractor, or a nonprofit workforce provider. Seek qualified discovery **before** public procurement. The question is whether an existing service provider can sponsor a small service agreement; do not assume it can direct-award or that program funds automatically cover iPurpose.

**Procurement**: Georgia DOAS currently directs suppliers to **GA@WORK Marketplace for Suppliers** for registration and opportunity notices, and the Georgia Procurement Registry for current bids: https://doas.ga.gov/state-purchasing/how-to/how-to-get-a-contract-with-the-state . Prepare business registration, Tax ID, official business contact, service-specific NIGP codes. Search related descriptions: career services, workforce development, training, coaching, education services, case management, digital learning, AI readiness (map to true service codes, no invented code). Note GA@WORK live transition, do not rely on stale Team Georgia Marketplace wording.

**WIOA Eligible Training Provider List**: for **occupational skills training** paid through Individual Training Accounts, WorkSource Atlanta has application/approval requirements (https://worksourceatlanta.org/training-providers/). iPurpose's current noncredentialed coaching/reflection pilot must not claim to be an approved occupational training provider or automatically WIOA-fundable. Ask whether it fits a contracted career-service activity or must pursue ETPL/another procurement route.

**Lower-friction distribution:** partner with an established approved nonprofit workforce contractor as a separately contracted adjunct (after approvals); the partner retains referrals and client governance and may have its own purchasing policies. Never bypass public procurement restrictions.
