# iPurpose | Facilitator Guide — Weeks 1 & 2 (DRAFT)
**Decision-to-Action | Adult cohort, 18+ | 60 minutes per week | Version 0.1, 2026-11-17**
**Not approved or licensed.** Review with an education/workforce program professional, accessibility reviewer and relevant institutional partner before external use. No counseling, diagnosis, psychotherapy, legal advice, occupational placement or psychometric claim. Participants should never feel compelled to disclose sensitive identity, finances, health, or employment records.

## Before delivery
**Materials:** participant-owned Decision-to-Action Plan v0.1; non-diagnostic five-statement check-in (provided separately); paper or institution-approved digital copies; timer; partner's support and referral contact; optional text-only examples. Digital tools, including AI, are **not required** in Weeks 1 or 2.

**Facilitator boundary:** Explain this is guided reflection and practical planning. People can skip a question, keep private notes, choose not to speak, or leave without penalty. Never infer psychological wellbeing or employment eligibility from responses. If students/clients request licensed counseling or high-stakes help, refer to the organization's designated professional, rather than giving clinical or legal advice. Gather no identifiable reflections unless institution has approved collection and a lawful basis.

### Week 1 — Find the next decision
**Purpose:** Shift from an abstract wish ("figure out my life") to one participant-owned, manageable decision. **Learning outcome:** By the end, each learner can articulate one decision, one reliable source of information, and one support person or service.

**60-minute session plan:**
- 0–5 | Welcome and boundaries. Explain three layers: *Soul = what matters; Systems = how I move; AI = optional help after clarity*. There is no "correct" purpose and no label the program assigns.
- 5–10 | Quiet check-in. Participants rate five original statements 1–5 for their own reflection. Do not display names or individual answers. The organization determines if an approved anonymous aggregate is gathered.
- 10–20 | Decision inventory. Prompt: "What is one choice, transition, or next step you keep thinking about?" Choose a low-to-moderate-stakes decision; avoid disclosure of private crisis, health, finances or sensitive circumstances. Example: "Which two training programs should I compare?"
- 20–30 | Agency exercise. Each learner makes three columns: *I can decide / I can influence / I need help or information*. Transfer chosen decision to first column if within their control, otherwise identify a smaller influence action.
- 30–40 | Reality check. Pair participants voluntarily, or offer silent independent work. Ask: "What information would make this decision easier? Which official source or qualified person could I consult?" Learners record one evidence source.
- 40–50 | Action scaffold. Complete plan fields: "Decision I'm exploring", "Why now?", "One missing fact", "One support contact", "A 14-day information-gathering step".
- 50–57 | Optional share-out: only the action, not personal history. Facilitator focuses on clarity of action, not suitability of participant's life choice.
- 57–60 | Exit ticket: "What is your next step, and when will you do it?" Learner keeps own copy. Staff share next week's session time and any approved support route.

**Facilitator questions:** Is the action chosen by the participant? Is it actionable in two weeks? Is the information source credible and accessible? Does the participant understand they can change direction later?

**Between-session task:** Gather one piece of reliable information relevant to the decision and note its source/date. Participants without digital access may use program staff, library and printed materials. Don't require AI.

### Week 2 — Understand the values behind a choice
**Purpose:** Clarify what "fit" means to the participant without assigning a personality type, career destiny or psychological diagnosis. **Learning outcome:** Each learner can name 2–3 decision criteria and explain how they might compare two options.

**60-minute session plan:**
- 0–5 | Check-in and choice rights. Remind learners they may skip private questions and need not reveal identity narratives.
- 5–15 | Strengths in context. Think of a task or activity where the learner felt capable or interested. Prompt: "What specific behavior helped you succeed?" Not "What are you born to do?"
- 15–25 | Constraints and needs. Privately list time, money, access, family responsibilities or prerequisites as relevant **without detailed disclosure**. Encourage fact checking rather than comparing yourself to someone else.
- 25–35 | Choose criteria. Select 2–3: opportunity to learn, work environment, meaning, income stability, realistic access, skill fit, flexibility, community contribution, or a learner-generated criterion. No one criterion is universally best.
- 35–45 | Compare two options. Using the participant plan, write a short plus/unknown for each criterion and each option. If learners only have one option, brainstorm a reversible alternative.
- 45–53 | Make one temporary, reversible decision about which option to explore first. Explain why it currently fits and what could change the judgment.
- 53–58 | Independent action update. Record one fact to verify and one 14-day action. Invite optional peer feedback; no public pressure.
- 58–60 | Closing: "What question will you bring to a qualified adviser or program coach?" Point to approved support channel.

**Facilitator questions:** Do not praise a specific choice as objectively correct. Verify that each choice reflects learner agency and practical constraints. Make room for disagreement, uncertainty, multiple careers and changing priorities. If sensitive concerns arise, refer privately.

**Between-session task:** Research two options using institutional/adviser-approved sources; note who verified them. Do not upload private educational, financial, medical or counseling information into generative AI.

## Instructor implementation checklist
- [ ] Approved plain-language accessibility accommodations (print, screen reader, low bandwidth)
- [ ] Adult-only participation and voluntary participation confirmed
- [ ] Institution-approved support/referral contact announced
- [ ] No private reflection collected without authorization
- [ ] Activity and worksheet fit existing program schedule
- [ ] Student may export/retain personal plan
- [ ] Learning evidence limited to approved aggregate completion/action counts
- [ ] Feedback is about usefulness and action, not diagnosing the participant

## Quality questions for first partner review
1. Would one trained staff member be able to deliver this without the founder?
2. What content duplicates your existing instruction?
3. Which participant groups need adaptations or alternative formats?
4. Which outputs are meaningful enough to count toward your program's existing goals?
5. What staff time, compliance and reporting requirements would make licensing impractical?

**Revision gate:** Partner reviewer should approve usefulness and workload of Weeks 1–2 before full content investment.
